Platform reliability

Risk-based validation for Databricks upgrades

Read the article ↗ Read the post ↗

Problem

An org-wide SQL warehouse upgrade could not depend on re-running all ~1,800 DAGs in UAT. Testing was ad hoc, slow, and costly.

Approach

A 3-tier framework: ~150 EXPLAIN probes that scan the whole repo, pattern-cover job manifests, and per-domain test lists owned by recent contributors. Wired into GitHub Actions with a runbook for every team.

Outcome

  • Time: saves engineers about 2 weeks of testing on every upgrade, with full-repo compile coverage in about 5 minutes.
  • Shared publicly: I published the approach as a LinkedIn article, so any team on a Databricks SQL warehouse can test its jobs' syntax and functions against a new version without running the jobs.
  • Cost: avoids about $10K in compute for every full UAT test run.
  • Onboarding: engineers new to the platform can check upgrade readiness themselves, guided by the runbook.
AI enablement · Knowledge sharing

PDI toolkit: an AI assistant for PySpark Data Integration

Problem

PDI (PySpark Data Integration), our metadata-driven ETL framework, runs critical warehouse loads, but few engineers knew how it works: load pointers, task wiring, metadata promotion, and reloads. Every change or production issue waited on a handful of experts.

Approach

Captured how the framework actually behaves as structured context for an AI coding assistant, packaged as a plugin. Engineers ask questions in plain language and get guided steps for runtime debugging, task changes, promotion, reloads, and upgrade checks.

Outcome

  • Self-serve: any engineer can learn the framework, make changes, and promote them without waiting on an expert.
  • Resilience: less dependence on single people and faster onboarding for new engineers.

Modernization · Cost & speed

Survey platform migration to Databricks

Read the post ↗

Problem

The survey distribution pipeline was split across Presto, Hive, a dedicated Postgres database, SFTP, and ActiveBatch scripts. Every change touched several systems, failures were hard to trace, and holiday pauses needed manual ops requests during code freezes.

Approach

Led the migration into one modular PySpark notebook on Databricks with direct source access, exclusion rules and holiday pauses in code, and monitoring and alerts for Operations. Moved three supporting jobs into the data quality and L3 layers.

Outcome

  • Time: change requests went from 4+ weeks to under 1 week, and 10+ were delivered after launch.
  • Money: the legacy Postgres database was decommissioned, saving about $3K a year in infrastructure, and the moved jobs cut compute further.
  • Decisions: surveys now reach unhandled calls too, so managers got reports on mishandled interactions and used them to target coaching.
Observability

Survey pipeline health dashboard

Problem

Survey runs depended on many upstream sources. A stale table, a drop in row counts, or a failure alert that never sent could stop surveys silently, and the business often noticed first.

Approach

Built a monitoring dashboard that tracks source row counts and refresh delays, grouped by survey program. Fixed the gaps in failure alerts and folded many alert emails into one notification backed by data quality checks.

Outcome

Engineers and Operations see the pipeline's health at a glance and catch source issues before surveys go out. It improved the platform's stability and reliability and cut time spent chasing failures.

Engineering standards

Scoring 3,800+ SQL jobs for optimization

Problem

Thousands of reporting SQL jobs had no shared view of which ones carried the most performance and correctness risk.

Approach

Built an automated scan that scores every L3 job on anti-patterns such as SELECT *, cross-join risk, DISTINCT-heavy logic, and missing date filters.

Outcome

A ranked remediation list that now guides upgrade planning and cleanup priorities. Recognized with a Divine Discontent award.

AI-assisted engineering

Teaching AI to write production-grade SQL

Read the post ↗

Problem

AI coding assistants write SQL that works but stops at "good enough": unneeded column scans, missed partition pruning, overused DISTINCT, inefficient joins, and ignored data skew.

Approach

Wrote our SQL optimization standards as a reusable rules file for the AI assistant: push filters early, prune partitions, select only needed columns, avoid shuffles, and judge the execution plan, not just the syntax.

Outcome

AI-generated queries follow the same standards as senior-reviewed code for Databricks and Spark. The rules are versioned and improve as best practices evolve.

How I work

Engineering principles

I look past the ticket for the bottleneck behind it, then fix it with a standard, an automation, or a better design.

Standards that scale

ANSI joins, atomic Delta writes, naming and lineage conventions. A pattern written once and reviewed everywhere beats a hundred clever one-offs.

Release discipline

Config-driven deployments promoted dev → UAT → prod through CI/CD, with downstream readers checked before anything ships.

Root cause, not workarounds

SLA misses and Spark failures traced through logs and query plans until the cause is fixed and written into a runbook.

AI-augmented, still governed

Cursor and generative AI speed up how I build and review, without loosening reliability, security, or data governance.

Grow other engineers

I have led teams of up to 13 and mentor and train engineers at every level, onshore and offshore, on pipelines, data quality, extracts, and PDI, through pairing, code reviews, and knowledge-transfer sessions.

Write the docs I wish I'd had

When I took over the survey platform, much of how it worked was undocumented, and I had to piece it together myself. Now I document what I learn, through runbooks, process guides, and Confluence pages, so the next engineer doesn't start where I did. With toolkits and AI assistants, Operations and new engineers can support and change systems without waiting on one expert.
